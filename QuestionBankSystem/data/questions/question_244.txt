Question:
In Rabin-Karp, why is the modulus M chosen as a large prime number?

Options:
A. To distribute hash values uniformly across the range [0, M-1] and minimize collisions
B. Because binary arithmetic only works with primes
C. To prevent floating point inaccuracies
D. To force all hashes to be positive even numbers

Correct Answer: A

Explanation:
A large prime modulus ensures uniform modular distribution and prevents common integer factors from concentrating hash values into small collision clusters.
