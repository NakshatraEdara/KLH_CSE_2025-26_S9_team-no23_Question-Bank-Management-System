Question:
Why MUST the intermediate vertex loop k be the outermost loop in the Floyd-Warshall implementation?

Options:
A. To ensure subproblems using intermediate vertices {1..k-1} are fully solved before considering vertex k as an intermediate candidate
B. It is arbitrary; the three loops can be ordered in any permutation without changing the result
C. To prevent null pointer exceptions
D. Because k represents the number of edges

Correct Answer: A

Explanation:
The DP state definition relies on k being incremented level by level. Placing k as the inner loop fails to consider paths where an intermediate vertex requires previously updated combinations.
