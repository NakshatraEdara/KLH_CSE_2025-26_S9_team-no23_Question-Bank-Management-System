Question:
How can one find the Lowest Common Ancestor (LCA) of two distinct keys u and v (where u < v) in a BST?

Options:
A. Traverse from root; the first node with key x such that u <= x <= v is the LCA
B. Find the mid-point of in-order traversal array
C. Calculate the average of u and v and search for that key
D. Run BFS from both nodes until they collide

Correct Answer: A

Explanation:
Because of the BST ordering property, if both u and v are smaller than current node, LCA is in left subtree; if both are greater, in right subtree. The first node between u and v splits them and is the LCA.
