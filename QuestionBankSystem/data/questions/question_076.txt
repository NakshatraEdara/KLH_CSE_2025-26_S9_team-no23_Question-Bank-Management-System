Question:
Why is a prime number commonly chosen as the table size m in hash tables with modular hashing?

Options:
A. To reduce clustering when keys share common divisors with table size
B. Because computer processors can only divide by prime numbers
C. To prevent memory leaks
D. To force all elements into index 1

Correct Answer: A

Explanation:
A prime modulus ensures that common patterns or strides in input keys (such as multiples of powers of two) do not bias hash results toward a small subset of slots.
